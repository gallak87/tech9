# Art pass

Follow the [game rules](../../../AGENTS.md), [art direction](../../../docs/ART_BIBLE.md) and assigned brief. Choose the creative approach freely; record and preserve what the user enjoys unless they ask to revisit it.

- At the start of an assigned art pass, create a goal covering the assigned map, visual improvements and final verification, provenance and preview updates. Reuse an existing goal for the same pass. Mark it complete once all required work is finished; the user need not separately request goal setup.
- Before baseline review, locate the map's original imagegen concept images and prompt in source assets/provenance, including its unique feature. Read the prompt and visually inspect the actual images. Give these references to the critic and modeler, using them to assess composition, forms, materials, lighting and the map's identity. Later user-approved direction takes precedence.
- Judge overall environment quality and racing presentation, giving racing impact priority. Weight features by prominence, exposure and experiential importance: small, rarely visible defects matter less; brief signature moments can matter greatly. Evaluate moving elements across their encounter. Use free camera for diagnosis.
- Establish matching baseline views and a concrete visual target. Use an independent, brutal AAA art critic, calibrated to the intended style and references. Keep its rubric stable, distinguishing composition/racing presentation, large forms and material/finish quality. At baseline, major milestones and completion, request one whole-course score out of ten, a brief assessment of those dimensions, wins to retain and the one or two weaknesses whose improvement would most benefit the experience. Calibrate anchors to the brief: roughly 5 means coherent with major weaknesses, 7 means strong with substantial gaps, and 9 means cohesive and convincing across representative racing views. Explain what holds the overall score back, including when a component is already strong; avoid implying precision unsupported by the images. The critic sets priorities; the artist chooses solutions. Score visible results.
- Between milestones, prefer a local keep/revise/reject verdict with the visible change and its significance over another numeric score. These focused reviews still count as rounds; broaden review when the candidate changes the course's overall impression.
- Address the dominant weakness with a coherent candidate, combining form, placement, lighting and materials where useful. Name the affected racing view or encounter and the improvement you expect the player to perceive. Detail includes convincing construction, attachment and material transitions. Require a clear improvement at normal racing size before spreading a treatment.
- Use Blender for substantial modeling or sculpting. Rebuild, replace or rescale weak forms as needed within the brief. Preserve editable sources and assess the exported result in-game.
- Move into finish work when the main remaining weaknesses are finish-related. Honor explicit phase counts; otherwise direct remaining rounds toward the largest unresolved weakness. After two marginal rounds, reassess the target or diagnosis and consider a different creative direction. Preserve established wins while exploring solutions.
- Keep trials cheap: prototype before expensive exports or bakes, and compare a few matched affected views. Review an ordinary stretch as well as a landmark. Broaden coverage at baseline, major milestones and completion; run checks appropriate to the change.
- Default to up to **16 improvement-and-review rounds**, stopping at **9/10 or higher** or the round cap, whichever comes first. The cap is a ceiling; absent an explicit required count, an unresolved plateau can justify concluding earlier with the remaining limitations explained. Baseline review is separate. Honor user overrides and keep the best result. Finish verification, provenance and preview updates under the game rules. Store only each map's latest numeric score in [ART_SCORES.json](../../../docs/ART_SCORES.json); keep detailed findings in chat. Completed passes stay closed.
