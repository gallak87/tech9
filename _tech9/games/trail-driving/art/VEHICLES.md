# Editable vehicles and driving feedback

The three vehicle assets are authored directly in [`src/vehicles/VehicleModel.js`](../src/vehicles/VehicleModel.js). This is the canonical editable model source: metre-scale Three.js primitives, rounded body panels, material palette, labels, wheel tread, rims, spokes, mechanical assemblies, interior, rider and articulation. There are no opaque generated model files or external model/texture dependencies. All vehicle designs and synthesized audio are original work created for Trail Driving and may be used, edited and redistributed with this project; no additional third-party asset attribution is required. The underlying Three.js dependency retains its MIT license.

`createVehicleModel('jeep' | 'atv' | 'bike')` returns `{group, update(snapshot, dt), mountedPose(), setDriverView(boolean), dispose()}`. Attach `group` to the scene; update it after `VehicleMotion` and before camera/render updates. The model copies wheelbase, track, radius and suspension limits from the driving configuration. Its origin is the ground baseline, forward is -Z, and YXZ chassis rotation applies `-heading`, `pitch`, `roll + lean`. World wheel centers are transformed from the actual terrain contacts, so chassis motion cannot detach the wheels from their sampled ground positions. Steering and wheel rotation follow the motion snapshot; suspension endpoints follow each wheel. Driver limbs remain connected to the hand controls, and rider posture responds to terrain and airtime. The driver's head is hidden in the mounted camera. Its pose retains visible foreground controls and interpolates chassis roll toward world-up for readable sightlines.

The ochre Jeep has seven grille slots, round headlamps, framed windshield and wipers, open cabin, dashboard, steering wheel, seats, roll cage, mirrors, step rails, spare wheel and expedition can. The teal ATV has open suspension, cargo racks, independent chunky tires, an exposed engine, foot platforms and steered handlebars. The cream/red bike has narrow spoke wheels, exposed fork and swingarm, engine fins, exhaust, tank, race-number panels, high fenders and the rider's moving posture. Numeric race markings are geometry, not raster textures.

Static primitives are merged by material while animated groups remain separate. At construction the Jeep is approximately 40,000 triangles / 70 meshes, ATV 27,100 / 64 and bike 21,400 / 52. Each model owns and releases its geometry and materials. These are model counts, not a whole-scene performance claim.

[`src/vehicles/DrivingFeedback.js`](../src/vehicles/DrivingFeedback.js) owns a fixed 128-particle surface dust pool and one local Web Audio graph. Construct with `{scene, camera}`; call `start()` directly from a player gesture, then `update(snapshot, dt)`. A supplied camera positions and orients the audio listener. The audio is synthesized from oscillators and filtered generated noise, with different engine registers for each vehicle, surface tire noise, braking friction and bounded landing/collision pulses. No sound recordings are required. `setVolume(0)` mutes, and `dispose()` closes audio and releases particles. Supply `active:false` or `paused:true` during pause, garage or results to silence the engine and stop emission. Existing particles fade naturally.

CPU verification exercised construction, all three sloped-terrain contact transforms (world-space error below 1e-8 m), finite mounted camera poses, repeated idempotent disposal, bounded particle emission and scene cleanup. Root integration owns browser screenshots, input verification and visual review.
