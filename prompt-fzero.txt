# Goal

Build an original, high-octane, fast-paced, futuristic anti-gravity racing game inspired by the blistering speed, precision and high-stakes racing of F-Zero. Working title: VECTOR//NOVA. Target the browser. Three.js + Vite + ES modules is a strong default, but choose the engine, rendering stack and asset pipeline that best serve the result. Babylon.js, WebGPU where supported, Blender-authored assets, procedural modelling and other appropriate tools are welcome. Explain the choice, then build the game.

Build it as a standalone application in its own directory, such as games/vector-nova/, with its own package manifest, dependencies, runtime, scripts and asset pipeline. This repository contains two competing frameworks: the root tech9 framework and _tech9/. Do not adopt either framework, scaffold the game through either one, or depend on their runtime, launcher, module registry, sync tooling or build conventions. The existing projects below are references from which to borrow selected techniques or self-contained code, not a required architecture. The finished application must install, run and build independently from its own directory.

Treat this as a real AAA production with a focused, finishable scope. Deliver a complete, polished racing experience with coherent art direction, excellent handling and replay value. AAA is the quality bar for everything the player touches; it is not permission to promise an impossible quantity of unfinished content. Use original ships, tracks, names, branding, music and UI. Capture the genre's exhilaration without copying Nintendo assets or recreating its specific designs.

The visual target is premium contemporary science-fiction realism: beautifully designed hard-surface racing machines, physically based materials, convincing lighting, dense but readable environments, dramatic scale, deep perspective and strong atmospheric depth. No pixel art, painterly textures, flat low-poly stand-ins, toy proportions or generic neon primitives presented as finished art. Futurism must come from industrial design, architecture, technology and worldbuilding, not just emissive strips and bloom.

The first deliverable is a small, playable staging environment for my review of the art direction and racing feel. Show me one proposed ship silhouette in a representative 3D racing level as early as possible, then wait for my explicit approval before committing to that direction or expanding production. The full-game requirements below are the destination after that review, not prerequisites for showing me the first build.

The finished game must include:

- A complete racing loop: polished launch screen, ship selection and garage, track selection, race briefing, grid introduction, countdown, racing, finish, results, rewards, retry and championship progression. Include Quick Race, a three-race Championship and Time Trial with a local personal-best ghost. Save settings, records and unlocks locally, with graceful handling when storage is unavailable. No accounts or backend required.
- Three complete, distinctive circuits with different racing identities: a layered megacity expressway at blue hour; and a sunlit orbital shipyard above a planet. Each needs authored sightlines, memorable landmarks, trackside engineering, alternate racing lines and a dramatic signature section. Deliver full circuits with meaningful corners and elevation changes, not variations of a flat oval. Aim for roughly 60–120-second laps at race pace after playtesting.
- Real three-dimensional racing: banked sweepers, drops, climbs, tunnels, elevated intersections and selected wall rides, corkscrews or loops. Build these around a stable local track frame so the ship, collision, gravity and camera agree through every orientation. The route must feel physically constructed in a larger world, with supports, barriers, undersides, service structures and believable connections. Avoid visible scenery cutoffs and empty space immediately beyond the road.
- Four finished, selectable ships with distinct silhouettes, handling and sound: a nimble interceptor, a balanced all-rounder, a heavy bruiser and a volatile speed specialist. Give each a readable cockpit/canopy, layered hull panels, credible propulsion, material variation, visible hover hardware, animation, thruster effects and restrained wear. Differences in acceleration, maximum speed, grip, mass, energy and recovery must matter in play. Ship previews must show the actual game assets.
- A field of at least 12 racers including the player, with competent AI that follows the real track, brakes for corners, chooses lines, overtakes, avoids hazards, uses boost and makes recoverable mistakes. Include distinct rival tendencies and difficulty levels. Any catch-up assistance must be bounded and documented; opponents must not teleport or ignore the handling rules to manufacture a close race.
- High-octane, fast-paced racing with snappy, expressive handling: immediate steering response, stable high-speed control, independent airbraking or lateral lean, controlled slip, readable traction recovery, satisfying wall contact and a skill-based side attack. Build sustained intensity through close rival battles, rapid corner-to-corner decisions, daring overtakes and explosive but controllable boost exits, with enough visual anticipation to make mastery possible. Speed must come from movement through the world, track cadence, sound and camera behaviour together. Do not substitute excessive FOV, screen shake or particles for a convincing driving model. Make a single clean lap enjoyable before expanding content.
- A boost/energy risk-reward system: boosting consumes a shared energy reserve that also absorbs damage; recharge lanes reward deliberate line choice. Add readable boost strips, jumps, hazards and a small, coherent set of tactical pickups such as a brief shield, an energy cell and a charged impulse attack. Telegraph effects, enforce cooldowns and stacking rules, and preserve racing skill as the main determinant of success. Use a one-slot pickup system with a clear activation control. Keep Time Trial rules fixed, pickup-free and comparable between runs.
- Full racing feedback: engine load, hover resonance, airbrake effects, thruster expansion, speed-sensitive wind, sparks on contact, shield impacts, boost shockwaves, damage warnings, checkpoint timing, near-miss feedback and a strong finish-line payoff. Establish a visual hierarchy so the racing line and upcoming hazards remain legible at maximum speed. Every major event needs an appropriate sound and visual response.
- A finished presentation layer: restrained motorsport-inspired typography, crisp HUD, readable speed/energy/position/lap indicators, minimap, split times, clear pickup state and minimal racing-line assistance that can be disabled. Include smooth menus, proper loading and error states, pause/restart, complete keyboard menu navigation, gamepad support, remappable controls, dead-zone settings, volume controls, graphics presets and reduced-motion options. Support responsive mobile menus and usable touch racing controls with configurable size/handedness, safe-area spacing, orientation handling and clean input release on focus loss. Tune mobile rendering separately and report tested devices honestly. Explain controls before the first race. No decorative buttons that do nothing.
