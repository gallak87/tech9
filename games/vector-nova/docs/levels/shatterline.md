# Shatterline design reference

**Implemented on main; retained reference, reviewed 2026-10-11.** The sprint, long flights and both freely drivable Overturn tubes are in the game. The production/critique instructions below describe the original assignment, not an active loop to resume. Current behavior is documented in the [README](../../README.md) and [architecture](../../ARCHITECTURE.md); new work belongs in the [roadmap](../../ROADMAP.md) or a separately assigned change. This status does not assert a new critic score or full mobile acceptance.

Build a jump-heavy, one-way downhill race through a ruined planetary mass driver. Start on a high mountain launch platform, descend through shattered accelerator infrastructure, and finish at the receiving installation in the basin. **Three signature jumps each deliver about five seconds of real airtime**, with smaller jumps and substantial racing sections connecting them.

The selected [scene concept](../../assets/levels/shatterline/reference/shatterline-scene-v1.png) establishes world, scale, palette, materials and atmosphere. **It is visual direction, not a track blueprint.** Design the playable route, jump spacing, receiving decks and camera independently. Preserve the downhill speed fantasy and monumental broken rings without copying the image's geometry or apparent distances.

## Race identity and pacing

This is a fast downhill sprint with the player and the existing three physical rivals, plus solo practice. The destination stays downhill; there is no return leg. Broad arcs, gentle lateral offsets and banked approaches add steering and overtaking decisions while retaining the long-axis launch-rail identity. Momentum, approach line, boost use and clean landings determine pace. Shatterline scales each ship’s cruise target by 25% and boost target by 15%, with gentler loss of earned downhill speed. Preserve real braking, ship differences and ballistic arrivals; accommodate the resulting speed with physical receiving runway.

The approved direction is a sustained, steep mountainside descent with very few level roadways. The route opens with a roughly kilometre-long Summit Chute, a 2.15 km Service Corkscrew combining two unequal rotations, and a 2 km Overturn Descent with a sustained upside-down hold. Avalanche Sweep then carries two gaps through broad same-direction banked turns; Scissor Dive folds below an earlier roadway, runs on its side and ends in a longer offset transfer and steep exit dive. Supported pitches reach roughly 40–51 degrees while preserving all twelve jumps and their receiving decks. The terrain, accelerator axis and distant basin follow the same descent. Overturn now combines exposed corkscrews with two freely drivable 360° tubes, as specified below.

Use these as starting targets, then tune against actual driving:

- Roughly 20.5 km of timed route and 6.58 km of net descent, with ordinary grades around 10 degrees, several long 40-degree descents and a brisk competitive run. Avoid padding the course to hit a number.
- Use smooth roll transitions with a readable upright approach and departure; keep the long inverted hold over an open ravine. Foundations and camera clearances follow the actual rolled road, including beneath it during inversion. Use 50 m of usable road width. Receiving decks may flare wider; taper changes smoothly and keep geometry, collision and AI bounds aligned.
- Aim for 12–16 meaningful jumps distributed through the run, including the three long flights below. Introduce the first ordinary jump within roughly 15 seconds. Alternate short hops, descending transfers and longer leaps so repeated launches do not feel identical.
- Provide sustained driving between jumps. A receiving deck needs enough length to absorb the slowest and fastest expected arrivals, settle the craft, steer, overtake and prepare for the next launch. At intended race speed, most landings should leave several seconds of useful grounded control; the three hero landings need longer recovery and acceleration reaches.
- Design all mandatory crossings for every player ship and all three rivals. Normal preparation with available boost/devices must suffice. A missed pickup or depleted reserve must not create an unavoidable repeating recovery trap.

Jump density must come from a long, varied course with frequent deliberate transfers. Tiny stepping-stone landings and immediate relaunches after every touchdown do not meet the direction. Avoid a whole run of featureless full-throttle straights; the grounded sections need purposeful line choices and changing composition.

## Three signature long flights

These are the level's defining interactions, not optional shortcuts. Place them in separate early, middle and final districts, with normal racing and smaller jumps between them. **Target approximately five seconds from actual takeoff to supported landing at the intended approach speed for each.** A starting acceptance window is 4.5–5.5 seconds at the nominal launch condition; measure and report the practical range for all ships and realistic unboosted/boosted approaches. Flight duration must emerge from real trajectory geometry and physics, never a timer, teleport or target-seeking flight path.

At horizontal speeds around 125–230 m/s, five seconds implies roughly 625–1,150 m of horizontal travel. Use this only to establish world scale; solve each actual ramp pitch, launch speed, height difference and landing envelope in the shared flight model before committing art. Extend flight timeout and miss bounds where measured valid trajectories require it; a normal five-second flight must never be classified as a miss halfway through.

## Raceway construction and world finish

The installation is a mountain-scale electromagnetic launcher with a raceway integrated into its remaining infrastructure. Build physical depth and attachment throughout: broad graphite induction cassettes, titanium load frames, thick cream ceramic insulators, recessed coil laminations, immense foundations and smaller maintenance assemblies.

- **Running surface:** substantial charcoal plates with broad machined joints, occasional pale service inserts and coherent wear. Preserve visible surface relief under raking sunlight without harsh flicker at racing speed.
- **Boundaries:** low layered ceramic and metal containment shoulders with inset amber-white guidance. Flared receiving edges communicate width. Jump lips have real thickness, exposed internal construction and clean collision boundaries.
- **Undersides and supports:** deep launch spines, articulated load shoes and anchored mountain foundations. Broken spans show convincing torn structural sections. Surviving road does not float on decorative poles or intersect arbitrary terrain.
- **Accelerator crowns:** author intact, partially fractured and heavily collapsed families with different silhouettes and visible coil/insulator construction. Avoid identical toruses scattered along the road. The hero crown needs compelling geometry from approach, inside its aperture and after departure.
- **Terrain:** pale fractured ridge faces, large geological strata, varied scree shelves and distant basin topography. Nearby cliffs and rock silhouettes are modeled geometry. Use large forms and erosion before fine texture detail.
- **Ordinary stretches:** maintenance galleries, recessed equipment, exposed load paths, scarred rock cuts and grounded debris compose foreground and middle distance. Every connector must carry the world's identity and finish.

Use charcoal, chalk-white geology, titanium and warm cream ceramics, with sparse vermilion maintenance marks and localized cold blue-white induction light. Preserve material differences rather than coating everything in uniform metallic gloss. Do not import another level's roadway or portal kit as the new world's identity.

## Light and atmosphere

Match the selected drawing's low warm sun, pale open sky, long raking mountain shadows and immense atmospheric distance. Bright rock and ceramic retain shape; sheltered service structures have deliberate cool fill and warm practical light. Road and craft remain legible when turning toward or away from the sun.

Use local dust shedding from ruined structure, distant haze and sparse ground-level drift to reveal scale. Keep takeoffs, receiving decks and the central view clear. Accelerator illumination can build toward the final launch and wash nearby hardware, but physical propulsion comes from declared existing devices or explicitly authored launch geometry. Decorative energy effects do not secretly change handling.

Generate a separate sky panorama from the selected concept's lighting and atmosphere during production. It supplies distant sky/clouds; nearby mountains, crowns, track and basin installation remain real scene geometry. Save the exact prompt and provenance. Inspect horizon, seam, full-course exposure and loading/disposal. High and low retain the same silhouettes, route cues and world identity. Reduced motion removes intrusive dust motion, flicker and distortion while preserving gameplay information.

## Point to point race support

The current game wraps route sampling and counts two laps. **A true open sprint is part of this build's scope.** Extend the existing level/race contracts with an explicit opt-in course format; retain closed-circuit defaults. Reuse the shared session, race field, devices, countdown, HUD and replay systems instead of forking them or hard-coding the level name.

1. **Route endpoints:** define open-course sampling, negative start-grid staging, camera/AI lookahead and a supported finish runout. No finish-to-start seam, visible return road or automatic teleport to simulate closure. Update numerical route checks to test open endpoints while preserving closed-loop wrapping checks.
2. **Progress and finish:** require ordered checkpoints and validated jump transfers. Record each racer's first valid terminal crossing exactly once; keep timing through flight and automatic recovery. Separate any presentation of midair progress from checkpoint credit, and ensure the three long flights produce sensible standings without inventing finishes. Never credit a finish from an invalid skip or manual recovery.
3. **Devices and AI:** preserve independent physics, reserve, Vector/Aegis eligibility and Easy/Medium/Hard behavior. Remove wraparound assumptions for sprint lookahead and device rearming. Rivals actually launch, land or recover and reach the finish themselves; they cannot teleport across gaps or inherit the player's result.
4. **Finish experience:** place the finish well beyond the final landing. Latch the player's result, then smoothly slow the craft in a supported runout and keep the remaining field simulating until its own finishes. Show retained results and existing restart/return actions. Prevent driving off the route endpoint; do not use an instant stop at the line or restart the run automatically. Keep the existing circuits' continued two-lap driving behavior unchanged.
5. **UI and solo:** use clear sprint wording and course progress/elapsed time rather than a fake lap count. Show distinct start/finish markers on an open minimap. Solo practice starts the full descent and supports finish/retry; this does not introduce the deferred ghost Time Trial mode.
6. **Replay and recovery:** version the new course format and geometry/rules identities. Preserve deterministic pause/reset/replay, full-field snapshots and incompatible-replay rollback. Automatic missed-jump recovery counts elapsed-time loss and keeps the run valid; manual recovery makes it untimed. Recovery places each ship on a supported usable approach with enough space to continue.

Reuse the current flight/contact system and shared Rapier support where appropriate. Keep genuine gaps and reachable solid collision: deck lips/undersides, receiving barriers, accelerator hardware and nearby terrain. Preserve supported contact recovery and the existing detached crash camera. Long flights must not bypass collision, use invisible road or receive artificial landing attraction. Shared fixes require regression checks on the affected original circuits.

## Production and independent critique

Follow the established **selected imagegen concept → Blender geometry/materials → in-game iteration → independent critic** workflow. The historical process and Helion brief are preserved in Git at revision `4775ac0ff82ab8c96ed6aefd5acf0084d4f752a3`, under `docs/expansion/README.md` and `docs/expansion/helion/HANDOFF.md`. Their old branch assignments and commit/merge instructions are historical; current game instructions and this brief govern this build.

1. Build and validate the complete open-course blockout and sprint lifecycle, including all three long trajectories, representative smaller jumps, camera clearances and every ship's landing envelopes. Treat this as internal production work; continue into art without adding an intermediate human approval gate.
2. Build bespoke accelerator crowns, fractured structural assemblies, mountain masses and receiving installation as real editable Blender assets or reproducible generators. Export early and inspect in gameplay lighting. Road sweeps, repeating panels, small fixtures and suitable effects may remain procedural. Retain original concepts, source files, exact prompts and provenance.
3. Finish the whole desktop world, including ordinary connectors, near/off-axis views, high/low and reduced motion. Compare palette, material quality, depth, silhouette and scale with the selected drawing, while judging track geometry on actual playability.
4. Use **one separate independent critic subagent**, explicitly acting as a **brutal AAA environment-art, lighting and racing-experience critic**. Give it this brief, the selected drawing, a frozen production build and an owned evidence directory. It is read-only apart from ignored evidence, captures its own stills and drives short real-input segments. The owner may delegate independent implementation work in non-overlapping files and integrates all fixes. No nested critic delegation. Run browser/render passes sequentially on the same build.
5. Use one counter across environment, geometry, sky, lighting, flight and shared fixes. Stop at the first verified pass or **ten completed formal rounds**, including the first review. Every applicable category needs **at least 9/10**, evidence and no critical defect. Grade environment art; lighting/materials/depth; handling/flight/camera; route/readability; feedback/UI; and stability separately. Missing evidence is unverified, and the cap does not convert failures into a pass. Do not restart the count for an asset or new session within this assignment.
6. Defer all mobile, portrait, touch-emulation and mobile-landscape checks until the final human approval gate, including embedded CPU viewport cases. Present desktop work for human approval first; do not run those checks during production or critic rounds.
7. Present the complete playable level for human driving review with the actual critic outcome and limitations. A critic score is not human acceptance. Commit, merge or publish only when requested. Keep progress and defects in chat, raw captures under ignored `.experiments/`, and enduring APIs/art decisions in the existing docs.

The critic must witness **all three approximately five-second flights** from the normal racing camera, including approach, airborne travel, landing and departure. Check ordinary driving between them with equal care. Code presence, a beautiful sky or one hero screenshot cannot raise an unfinished world to 9/10. Reject flat/repeated accelerator forms, missing material relief, poorly supported road, clipped cameras, unreadable distant landings, tiny receiving platforms, arbitrary misses and long flights that feel like slow suspended cutscenes. Do not penalize the world for departing from the concept's exact road arrangement.

Ordinary trajectory coverage and race-completion tests are separate, purposeful checks; do not restart the contact budget each critic round.

- Measure takeoff-to-supported-landing time, displacement, arrival speed and remaining usable receiving runway for each hero jump. Verify nominal approximately five-second flights and practical approach envelopes across all four ships and rivals, including off-center, under-speed and boosted entries. Confirm timeout and miss bounds allow complete valid flights.
- Verify every authored jump's real takeoff/landing and checkpoint credit, plus representative short/long arrivals, rail rebounds, underside/structure collisions, failed-jump recovery and continued throttle/steering after landing. Recovery must not place a racer in an immediate second failure.
- Complete competitive sprints with independent results for the player and three rivals at each difficulty. Verify solo finish/retry, no duplicate finish, no endpoint wrap, invalid-run labeling and sensible standings during long flights. Use CPU or accelerated replay for full-course completion rather than normal-speed automated laps.
- Check deterministic replay across the long flights and recoveries, pause/resume in midair, reset before/after finish, ship changes, retained results, live input clearing and resource/asset failure cleanup.
- Inspect the whole production course from chase and shared free camera, including all three jump corridors, sheltered/bright transitions, underside/support connections and finish runout. Check high/low/reduced motion and real loading/frame-pacing issues.
- Run focused route, flight, sprint, AI, device and replay tests; `npm run check:level -- shatterline`; `npm run build`; and `VN_URL=<owned preview URL> npm run verify:level -- shatterline`. Adapt shared contract checks explicitly for open routes; do not weaken closed-circuit checks to make this course pass. Run affected original-level regressions after shared changes.
- Keep mobile/portrait coverage deferred at the final human approval gate; report that boundary without claiming it passed.

Deliver working production/shape preview links, concise verified outcomes, actual hero airtimes, the critic round count and remaining issues. Preserve the selected concept and editable sources. After human acceptance, retain enduring design/API changes in the art bible and technical docs and retire the completed execution brief according to the repository's documentation policy.
