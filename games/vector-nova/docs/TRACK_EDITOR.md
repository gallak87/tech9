# Track Workshop

Open the top-level **Editor** tab in the full game or browser build. The Road palette and **File → New track** offer four playable starters with scenery and pickups. **Harbor braid** links two high flyovers, a short wall ride and two jump transfers over 7.5 km. **Waterfront loop** is a compact 3 km sprint with short air. **Forest descent** drops nearly 600 m through two jumps. **Canyon jump practice** has two long transfers. Click the empty ground to draw on the 100 m grid. A starter creates a new draft in one undoable step. **Road → Create road** changes the road on the current draft. Choose a style and length, then select **Create road**. Stacked and Woven require Circuit. Downhill has a supported 600 m braking runout after the finish. Browser development also accepts `?editor=1` after career selection.

## Click, shape, drive

The 3D view is the editing surface. Choose Draw, Select / move, Height or Pan in the viewport toolbar or press D, V, H or P. The Point, Section and Jump tabs in the Inspector give access to coordinates, banking, jump endpoints and local starts. Focus, Overview, zoom, Fly, Map and Profile sit above the viewport.

A ghost point and road continuation preview show where the next Draw click will go, including over existing road. Moving a point or using its height arrow while drawing keeps Draw active, so you can continue placing points afterward. Circuit closes automatically; after four or more points, choose **Finish drawing** or click the starting point. The editor frames your road and enters Select / move. Click a road point and drag it to bend the route; Shift-drag or its height arrow changes elevation. Hold Alt for finer height movement; the live guide reports the height delta. Drag the roll grip to rotate the road about its tangent. Hold Alt for fine height or roll. A red section marks a rapid change of roll and remains available to drive. Double-click between handles in Select / move to insert a point. One Undo restores a complete drag.

A status strip below the workspace shows whether you can drive. A tight hairpin, steep crest, abrupt bank, low bridge or nearby deck can carry a driving note and still start Practice. Open **Warnings** and choose a point to focus it. A deck-clearance note identifies both stretches and shows their sampled vertical and horizontal separation. The check includes space around the ship and camera. **Auto fix corner** is optional. Undo restores a repair. **Spread road out** gives a plain drawn road more room.

The notched ruler beneath the viewport follows the road in point order. Its trace shows relative point heights and its red marker locates the current point. Scroll, scrub, use the arrows or type a point number to jump there. Both ends wrap. Shift-click two points to select the span between them, or tap **Select span** and its end point. Cmd/Ctrl-click toggles individual points. Drag any selected point or its height or roll grip to move the selection together. The **Height** and **Roll** labels under the ruler also scrub horizontally and accept an exact value in their adjacent fields. The controller D-pad adjusts the ruler and either grip.

For a progressive wall ride, choose a span and press a **Twist** arrow in the viewport strip. Choose 90°, 180° or 270° for the turn. **Straight** holds the starting angle through the section. On a single point the arrows find a usable forward span. The road retains its exit angle after the twist and circuits blend back to the start angle near the finish. The Inspector still has precise endpoints, road surfaces and coordinates.

The **Jumps** group offers Small / Medium / Large presets. Select a road point and **Preview new jump here**, or **Find clear space** to search the course. A translucent proposal labels its takeoff and landing and reports the actual gap, receiver and total footprint. **Apply jump** commits one undoable edit; Cancel or Escape leaves the road unchanged. Anchors are sampled between existing points, so sparse roads do not need extra points first. The requested size is a horizontal guide; the final curved gap can be longer. Advanced endpoints remain available for custom spans and huge jumps. Turn off ramp assistance to keep the exact shape of your road, including a banked takeoff or short approach. The gap still needs ordered endpoints and its own receiving deck.

New assisted jumps retain a bounded original-corridor snapshot. **Preview resize** restores that baseline before reshaping, avoiding accumulated ramp changes; Apply keeps the same jump ID. Editing the generated ramp manually requires rebuilding explicitly. Older/generated jumps use **Preview rebuild**, adopting their current road as the first baseline; their discarded historical road cannot be reconstructed. Cruise/boost forecasts use the selected stock ship and shared ballistic engine. They are reference conditions, not minimum-speed guarantees, and omit scenery collisions and steering. Structural road checks and flight advice are separate.

Jump lips stay anchored to road points while other sections change. Removing a gap restores the road but retains its ramp/landing point edits. Undo restores the complete previous edit. Generating replaces the route and its jumps; changing course type clears incompatible features. Both operations remain undoable.

**Create road** offers styles and lengths from 3 to 20 km. Curviness shapes the racing line. Vertical variation (Descent strength on Downhill) shapes the height profile. Section frequency controls the spacing of carves, crests and dives. Banking controls deck rotation. Circuit Crossovers adds grade-separated bridges. Jump frequency and size reserve space for approaches, gaps and landings. Choose **None** to omit jumps. **Seed & pickups** holds the reproducible road seed and pickup fill.

Choose **Create road** to apply settings or **Try another variation** for a fresh seed in one undoable edit. New roads start upright and return toward level between banked carves. At high Banking, a long Woven circuit can fit a full corkscrew between jumps. The takeoff and receiving deck stay close to level in roll, so a jump can still climb or drop. Requested downhill length includes 600 m of runout. Existing files load their authored geometry, and road seeds generated with versions 1–6 can still reproduce their original geometry.

## Around your road

The left palette has **Road**, **Scenery** and **Pickups** tabs. Road shows starters and route anchors. Choose Scenery to browse biome swatches and apply an environment to the current route. The tabs share the draft, camera and Undo history.

The scenery picker offers 17 world families, including Oriel and Worldspire. Each family has curated character presets, a corresponding road kit and its registered sky and lighting. These are seeded environments built around the player's road.

Nightweir retains **Waterfront**, **Industrial basin** and **Reclaimed waterfront**. Its regional plan fills the course and surrounding space with connected terrain, rivers, basins, founded districts, quays, working yards and parks. New environments use the game’s production buildings, forest, ice, islands, shipyards, observatories, mineral formations and machinery as complete seeded assemblies. Nacre adds ocean shelves, reef habitats and tidal observatories; suspended worlds keep their floating foundations. Nightweir uses complete occupied civic and working-waterfront buildings. Required road edges, supports and foundations stay automatic. Dry-world horizons surround the surveyed terrain; local depressions and their foundations use the actual terrain height, without an overlapping flat ground layer. The editor does not require arranging individual buildings or plants. Most surrounding art is decorative; selected structures and tree trunks have contact, while the full visible assembly is kept clear of the road and jump corridors. Active campaign hazards remain future work.

**Road seed** determines the next route. **Environment seed** determines scenery independently. Pick a biome and a thumbnail swatch, then choose **Apply environment**. Fullness and seed are under **Scenery settings**. **Try another variation** picks another environment seed and applies it in one undoable action. Both preserve road points, angles, jump anchors and pickups. The applied scenery fits itself around the road as you draw or generate it. Scenery Fullness adds more clusters along the road first and leaves the distant horizon more open. The placement checks reserve the road and flight corridors. A blank draft shows a general preview of the biome. Save stores the applied world.

Pickups fills the course with real Vector boosts, Aegis shields, Repulsors, Arc bursts and Ion wakes. Balanced/Boost heavy and spacing options offer a few useful variations. Clear and Undo restore the expected layouts. Devices follow the actual banked/inverted road surface and avoid jump corridors. Refill after substantial road changes to update safe placement.

**File**, **Save** and **Test drive** sit together in the header. File opens under its button with New track, saved tracks, Reset road, Import and Export. The left palette holds road creation, scenery swatches and pickup fill. The viewport toolbar keeps the tools and camera controls visible. The right **Inspector** contains Point, Road section and Jumps. On compact screens the palette and inspector open above the viewport with bounded, scrollable controls.

## Camera, controls and testing

Drag the open 3D canvas to pan on the X/Z plane. Choose **Pan** (P) to drag anywhere without editing. Drag a handle to edit the track. In Draw mode, click to place a point and drag the open canvas to pan. Right-drag or Space-drag orbits, and the mouse wheel zooms. Middle-drag and Shift-right-drag also pan. New generation frames the complete layout. Loading long courses initially frames their first 1.5 km. Overview frames the entire route. Focus selection (or F) brings the selected point or section close enough to edit. Ordinary Undo/Redo keeps your camera in place. Restoring a replaced route reframes it. Map opens an interactive top-down view. **Profile** plots road elevation against distance, marks jump gaps and downhill finish/runout, and shares point selection with the 3D view. Both open over the canvas and close with their close button, an outside click or focus change, or Escape/B. Fly camera enables WASD and Q/E movement, drag look and Shift for speed. A standard controller uses left stick movement, right stick look and shoulder buttons for elevation. Escape/B leaves free camera and returns focus to its launcher.

Native header, palette, toolbar and inspector controls provide keyboard/controller equivalents for editing gestures. The permanent camera controls offer Focus, Overview, Zoom in/out, Fly, Map and Profile. Mouse gestures handle orbit and pan; Fly provides keyboard/controller movement and look. Tab/Shift-Tab moves through visible controls and keeps focus in view; Enter/Space activates. Controller D-pad/stick moves through the same controls, A activates, and left/right adjusts native selectors/sliders. Escape/B leaves free camera or closes open controls first, then stops Draw and clears the current point or section selection without removing road points. A pending jump preview cancels before closing its group. A subsequent press while idle returns from the editor; holding Escape does not repeat those steps. The authoring modes and camera controls remain visible while options expand or collapse. Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes, Shift+Ctrl/Cmd+Z redoes, and Ctrl/Cmd+S saves. Physical Steam Input/device checks remain separate release validation.

Open **Warnings** in the status strip to see measured bends, grades, rotation, clearance and jump checks. Select a listed point to find it on the road. Verified local repairs appear when available and can be undone. Driving notes permit Test Drive. Issues under **Before driving** show what the route needs before Practice can start. Drafts remain editable and saveable.

**Test drive** starts stock-ship Practice at the beginning of the course. The Point Inspector offers **Test from here**, and Jumps offers **Test this jump** from its supported approach. Both retain entry speed and reserve under **Test options**. Use **R** or **Pause → Restart** to repeat the current start with fresh physics and devices. **Pause → Back to editor** restores the draft, selection and history. Tests on the same course and ship reuse a suspended Practice session. Custom tracks skip campaign rewards and official Time Trial records.

## Saves and limits

**Save** adds a level to the local **Custom levels** library. Quick Race, Practice and Time Trial use that tab in Race setup. Choose **Create level** to build one or **Edit level** to reopen a selection. Incomplete drafts can be saved and finished later. **File** opens saved levels and provides Save a copy, Delete, Import and Export. Leaving/reopening the editor in the same frontend retains the draft. Applied edits also checkpoint to a separate browser recovery slot after 700 ms and on page hide/exit. After refresh, **Continue draft** restores it, or **Start fresh** discards the checkpoint. Resolve that choice before authoring. Explicit Save updates the library independently of recovery. Pending environment controls are not part of the applied recovery document. A storage failure keeps the previous checkpoint and reports the need to Save/Export. Import changes the draft and requires an explicit Save before changing the library. Failed writes and malformed files preserve the current document.

**File → Clear road** clears the road, jumps and pickups and returns to Draw. It retains the track name, identity and world settings. Undo restores the complete previous draft; Reset does not overwrite a saved track until you Save.

Browser builds use independent localStorage and file import/download. Electron uses atomic files in the full edition's `userData/tracks` directory and native `.vntrack.json` dialogs, separate from careers. Both accept the same track-file format, including all bundled biomes and assisted jump data. Libraries support 256 tracks and a 1 MB file limit. Steam Cloud/Workshop sharing is not configured.

Version-1 files store stable ID/name, a registered whole-course biome, road seed/options, independent versioned environment seed/layout, actual route points and unwrapped roll, world character/settings, anchored gaps and a resolved pickup layout. Legacy files without an environment block inherit their old seed and keep the previous roadside layout until explicit environment regeneration. New files use production scenery layout version 4. Saved layouts 0–3 keep their previous scenery until you explicitly regenerate the environment; Shatterline retains its production scenery introduced in layout 3. Regeneration upgrades the surroundings in one undoable action and preserves the authored road. Loading never regenerates saved road geometry. Unknown fields are dropped; unsupported versions/biomes/objects are rejected. Files contain no scripts, arbitrary asset URLs or filesystem paths.

One Circuit or Downhill course per file; up to 256 route points and 25 km of authored road. Generated lengths remain 1.8–20 km; the extra authoring room lets users reshape a 20 km starter without immediately invalidating it. Width is 32–64 m, elevation 0–3000 m, and bank trim about ±20°. Rotation chunks allow full inversions independently of trim. Branches, tubes, moving machinery, freeform wave curves and optional Freestyle trick scoring remain future work.

See [the editor roadmap](../track-editor-roadmap.md) for per-span mixed biomes, independent sky choices, optional props/hazards and later simulation features. The world selector still chooses the whole environment/backdrop. A bounded road-finish pilot in **Road section** uses the shared From/To points to apply Nightweir or Vesper surface materials. Endpoint IDs follow later road edits; Apply/Clear, Undo and file roundtrips preserve the road geometry and driving rules. Passive join bands mark finish boundaries. Jump/twist corridors are protected, and finish overrides must be cleared before deleting their endpoints. This pilot does not mix scenery or gameplay hazards; broader biome transitions remain on the roadmap.

Run `node tools/update-starter-outlines.mjs` after changing a starter recipe to refresh the palette’s route silhouettes.

## Verification and resource measurements

Run `node --test --test-concurrency=2 src/editor/*.test.js`, `npm run test:fast`, `npm run lint` and `npm run build -- --configLoader native`. Browser tools require an isolated `VN_URL` and launch muted by default. `editor-contexts-verify.mjs` checks independent seeds/history/save behavior; its `--water-clearance` branch checks water-preset validation and Undo. `editor-iteration-verify.mjs` (also `--responsive` and `--incomplete-recovery-only`) hand-draws a sparse course through real preview clicks, checks proposal/Apply/resize, recovery, finish spans and native local retries. `editor-spans-verify.mjs` checks both pilot directions, delayed texture readiness/reflections and cheap 3D dragging. `editor-biomes-verify.mjs` checks every family and legacy roundtrips, with a short `--responsive` branch for final touch coverage. `editor-tools-verify.mjs` and `editor-toolbar-verify.mjs` cover authoring, camera, Map and focus behavior. `VN_URL=… VN_TRACK=/path/to/course.vntrack.json node tools/editor-jump-camera-verify.mjs` observes native jump-camera handoffs from public point starts, current Downhill generation and exact Save/Undo; add `--responsive` only for final portrait coverage. `node --test tools/editor-camera.test.mjs` checks frame-rate/phase and lane continuity against the production camera rig.

`VN_URL=… node tools/editor-world-budget.mjs --layouts=1,2 --local-rebuild` compares the same Nightweir road/environment inputs and performs a native selected-point height edit. Add `--practice` with `VN_QUALITY=low|high` for matched warm Practice measurements. The fixtures use road seed 42, environment 7001, Waterfront fullness .82 and 20 km Crossovers 1. Texture estimates include owned shader maps.
