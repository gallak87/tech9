# Editable vehicles and driving feedback

The three vehicles are original editable procedural assets. [`src/vehicles/VehicleModel.js`](../src/vehicles/VehicleModel.js) authors the vehicle bodies, mechanical assemblies, controls, wheel construction, materials and articulation. [`VehicleGeometry.js`](../src/vehicles/VehicleGeometry.js) contains the explicit panel polygons, longitudinal loft construction, shaped tire cross-sections, curved tubes, deterministic surface-grain maps and original graphic texture generation. [`RiderModel.js`](../src/vehicles/RiderModel.js) owns the attached riders. These files are the canonical model source; no opaque generated models or downloaded model/texture dependencies are required. The vehicle designs and synthesized audio are original work created for Trail Driving and may be used, edited and redistributed with this project; no additional third-party asset attribution is required. Three.js retains its MIT license.

## Construction and finishes

The ochre Ridge Jeep uses a tapered bonnet with a center pressing and panel reveals, a rising cowl, stamped tub sides, flared wheel openings, thin gasketed windshield, optical headlamps, recessed grille, shaped recovery bumper and winch, formed seats, stitched upholstery, instruments, cage, mirrors, full spare and rear tailgate hardware. Its mounted controls include a functioning analog speedometer and padded three-spoke steering wheel.

The teal Scout ATV uses a formed nose and central shell, shaped front/rear wheel shoulders, a narrow saddle nose, separate tubular cargo racks, painted lamp bezels with recessed paired projectors, cooling apertures, an open lower frame and pierced foot platforms. Connected A-arms, half-shafts and coil-over assemblies follow the four wheel centers. The upper frame rails run inboard of the swept knees; forward engine covers and vents leave the shin corridor open. The rider controls include levers, pivot housings, switches, cables, ribbed grips, handguards and a live dashboard.

The cream/red Summit Bike uses a tapered tank, compound curved radiator shrouds, a long narrow gripper saddle, curved high fenders, a thin cupped headlamp mask and formed side number covers. A connected cradle frame surrounds separate cast engine cases and a compact finned cylinder, with radiator, skid plate, articulated rear swingarm, chain run and sprocket. The exhaust passes below the guarded crankcase and rises behind the heel; its oil line stays forward of the calf corridor. Front telescopic forks, crown and axle track the actual front wheel while the headlamp/fender assembly steers with the controls. Narrow wheels have crossed wire spokes and brake assemblies.

All tires use authored radial cross-sections with recessed beads, formed sidewalls, rounded shoulders and staggered restrained tread blocks. Rims have distinct lips, hubs, hardware and brake components. Body paint, molded plastic, powder-coated frame, cast steel, machined aluminum, rubber and woven textiles use different material responses. Painted surfaces have clearcoat and fine deterministic bump/roughness texture; rubber and textile maps use separate scale/patterns. Restrained dust color is baked onto low body/frame surfaces in authored vehicle coordinates, excluding riders and controls. Thin cupped handguards retain separate metal support spines and rolled edges. Small original canvas graphics provide model identity, registration, molded tire lettering, number markings and instruments; all tire labels within a model share one owned texture. Canvas artwork is generated from editable source at runtime and contains no third-party images.

## Runtime contract and ownership

`createVehicleModel('jeep' | 'atv' | 'bike')` returns `{group, update(snapshot, dt), mountedPose(), setDriverView(boolean), dispose()}`. Attach `group` to the scene; update it after `VehicleMotion` and before camera/render updates. The model copies wheelbase, track, radius and suspension limits from the driving configuration. Its origin is the ground baseline, forward is −Z, and YXZ chassis rotation applies `-heading`, `pitch`, `roll + lean`. World wheel centers are transformed from actual terrain contacts, so chassis motion cannot detach wheels from their sampled ground positions. Steering, wheel rotation and connected suspension follow the snapshot. Rider posture responds to terrain/airtime and hands follow the moving controls. Boot soles remain on the rendered foot supports while articulated uppers follow the shins; camera-specific forearm origins keep cut sleeve ends outside mounted views without moving the hand contacts. Head, torso, upper arms and marked obstructing parts are hidden for mounted views; foreground hands/controls remain.

Static meshes are merged by material, while animated, separately hideable, transparent and graphic surfaces retain their hierarchy. Every model owns its geometry, palette materials, generated texture maps and instrument resources. `dispose()` collects these resources by identity and releases each once, including repeated disposal safety. A renderer-supplied `envMap` is borrowed and is deliberately excluded from disposal; the game's vehicle lighting owner releases that shared reflection resource. No model replacement can retain its source canvas through a scene reference after cleanup.

## Feedback and verification

[`DrivingFeedback.js`](../src/vehicles/DrivingFeedback.js) owns a fixed 128-particle surface dust pool and one local Web Audio graph. Construct with `{scene, camera}`; call `start()` directly from a player gesture, then `update(snapshot, dt)`. A supplied camera positions and orients the audio listener. The audio is synthesized from oscillators and filtered generated noise, with different engine registers for each vehicle, surface tire noise, braking friction and bounded landing/collision pulses. No recordings are required. `setVolume(0)` mutes, and `dispose()` closes audio and releases particles. Supply `active:false` or `paused:true` during pause, garage or results to silence the engine and stop emission; existing particles fade.

`tests/vehicle-assets.test.js` checks all three models against sloped-terrain contact transforms, finite geometry/camera poses, exactly-once geometry/material/texture disposal, preservation of borrowed environment textures, and boot soles staying on rendered foot supports through representative poses. Correct rider/chassis fit also requires the fresh close side, steering and takeoff/landing captures; broad bounding boxes or distant chase screenshots alone are not proof of clearance. Root integration owns browser input verification, real-park inspection screenshots, movement sequences and the independent bounded art review. Review scores and current evidence are recorded in the game README; source construction details alone are not a claim of AAA quality.
