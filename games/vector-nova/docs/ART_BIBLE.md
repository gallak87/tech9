# Art direction

## Shared principles

The whole world and the raceway must share an identity: surface, edge silhouette, underside/supports, ordinary connectors, foreground, middle distance, sky and light. A strong hero vista cannot compensate for unfinished stretches.

Engage early and spread climbs, drops, banks, corkscrews and inversions through the lap, with readable recovery space. Do not stretch flat straights to meet a length target or cluster every dramatic feature in one short sequence.

Course challenge comes from harder obstacles or hazards and more consequential jumps or exposed edges where a fall is easier. Visual clutter and an unreadable route are not difficulty. A biome-road may alternate with constructed connectors or form the entire route; its material and edge cues must make the drivable surface clear.

Readability order: upcoming road/corner, craft/propulsion, gameplay feedback, decoration. Use real geometry, meaningful large/medium/small forms, coherent material response and grounded construction. Avoid bloom hiding form, screenshots posing as scenery, palette-swap levels and detail that vanishes at driving speed.

Match the concept's large spatial relationships before chasing surface detail: broken silhouettes, connected masses, recesses, scale and depth from the actual driving camera. Scanned textures do not turn smooth extrusions or swollen stacked forms into fractured geology. Strong materials and lighting should reveal good forms; tiny decorative noise cannot supply missing structure. Treat Worldkit as the reusable source of proven assets, not a reason to preserve an unsuccessful asset family. Establish a visibly convincing representative area before spreading it through a level.

Establish sky, key light and reflections together early. A generated panorama can give the whole lap a strong mood and reflected color; align the lighting with it while keeping road markings and hazards readable.

High/low presets retain the same world and navigation cues. Reduced motion restrains flashes and environmental movement. Use generated imagery only for appropriate asset/material roles, with exact provenance; preserve editable sources and licences.

Racing speed should read through peripheral travel and a widening view, with the upcoming road and player silhouette kept clear. The last 20% of cruise speed builds a subtle rush capped at one-third of boost blur; boost adds blue edge streaks and stronger rush, and Vector remains the strongest surge. Rivals carry visible nozzle-mounted blue thrust that grows under boost; Low keeps a simple fading silhouette. Pickups and outgoing shots give small kicks, received hits and collisions carry more weight, and heat adds a fine tremor. Responses settle quickly rather than sustaining heavy screen shake; reduced motion removes the added camera movement and speed lens.
