# Sound effects

Open the game with `?dev=1` or `?dev=2` to enable **Sounds**. It docks into the Ship inspector tools; wide menus also show it beside Tunes. On narrow screens, audition through the inspector to keep menu navigation clear. Pick a sound and press **Play**; picking another stops the current preview. Use **M** to pause Tunes while listening. Effects volume comes from Settings. Each preview stops automatically; engine and sustained effects play for three seconds.

These 35 choices are sorted alphabetically by group and name. Shared recordings appear once; volume-only variants and silent events have no separate entries. The Sounds panel uses the same generated samples, synthesis and effects volume as gameplay. It sets the output level before the first transient, keeps each decoded tail intact, and reports a missing sample instead of auditioning a substitute. The preview owns separate audio resources and does not activate pickups or alter the race. Audition from a menu or pause screen to hear the selected effect in isolation: racing audio and Tunes otherwise continue, and the isolated cue does not reproduce competition between voices in the full racing mix.

For pickup sound and VFX together, open **Ship inspector → Powerups, equipment & contact** with `dev=1` or `dev=2`, or go directly to `?inspect=1&dev=1&ship=kestrel`. Choose a device and press **Trigger**. The Arc, Repulsor and Aegis sound sets match gameplay. Aegis has separate full-duration and absorb/break sequences. Check **Shield target** to preview a blocked first hit, and **Engine sound** to hear Driving/Boost alongside the device.

Eliminator reuses the short start-light tone for its final three cut warnings and a quieter existing finisher sting when another racer is extracted. Player elimination uses the descending close; surviving the final cut uses the established winner sting. There is no second overlapping cut cue on the player’s terminal result, and continuous laps do not announce a final lap.

Interceptor's issued pursuit Lance retains the approved deep fire and compact impact samples at its mission firing cadence. The normal equipment item keeps its existing recharge. Ion countermeasure contact uses the existing incoming combat feedback; mission escape reuses the established descending-close cue and each boss has a dedicated victory quench.

Goliath has eight boss-only recordings: pressure charge, magnetic yaw, siege cannon discharge, exposed vent release, cannon energy recovery, critical core hit, target lane latch and disabled core quench. Deterministic `interceptor-cue` events select them through the race effects bus; cannon audio follows accepted physical firing. A pressure shot hitting the unshielded player triggers the recovery cue; confirmed player hits on the physically exposed core trigger the critical thud. The lane latch ducks the remaining charge body by 12 dB over 15 ms; recovery ducks that boss's active cannon tail by 6 dB over 15 ms. The quieter return reuses the magnetic turn. A real Goliath victory uses the quench instead of the generic winner cue and preserves its tail into results. Retry clears all active race one-shots while retaining the decoded samples. The Sounds picker exposes the same recordings under Interceptor · Goliath. Mastering preserves a low mechanical body, limits isolated peaks and quickly fades the release, with no changes to the approved player Lance samples. Current generation, edits and hashes live in `assets/audio/sfx/bosses/provenance.json`; human listening approval remains pending.

Wraith has its own eight recordings under Interceptor · Wraith: twin capacitor charge, ion lock, paired reverse thrust, ion scissors discharge, lock rupture counter, capacitor vent, critical capacitor hit and twin-drive quench. Its return reuses the quieter Wraith turn. Successful counters duck only that boss's active charge/lock/turn tails by 12 dB over 15 ms so the descending rupture reads clearly; player Lance retains its approved recording and envelope. Critical thuds require confirmed player weak-point hits on Wraith itself. The dedicated victory quench retains its complete release through results and obeys pause, mute, retry and disposal. These cues use the existing race effects bus and sample loader. Exact generation and mastering provenance is retained beside the WAVs; human listening approval remains pending.

Bulwark's eight recordings under Interceptor · Bulwark use a warm moving-vault identity: shield bulkhead load, low vault latch, heavy gyro turn, contained pressure slab, shoulder emitter rupture, shoulder shutters, critical shoulder thud and shield-collapse rundown. Phase cues follow the real breakwater timeline; return reuses its quieter 0.9-second turn. Only a confirmed player shoulder hit triggers the critical thud, and a real emitter rupture adds the counter release. That release ducks Bulwark's own warning, shoulder-thud and shutter tails by 12 dB over 15 ms; other bosses and player Lance retain their envelopes. The 1.4-second defeat release survives results and obeys pause, mute, retry and disposal. Generation remains sequential and exact provenance stays beside the masters; human listening approval remains pending.

The cup reward starts at least 0.6 seconds into its reveal and 0.25 seconds after any remaining race-finish voice. It follows effects volume, cancels when leaving results, and does not replay when reopening saved standings. Reduced motion keeps the trophy visible without the entrance animation.

The propulsion bed continuously responds to acceleration, braking, steering and speed; its three presets are representative states, not separate recordings. A shielded `combat-hit` is silent because `shield-hit` supplies the audible cue. Arc, Repulsor and Ion blocked-hit confirmations share the Aegis collapse recording. Expiry, cancellation and attacker confirmation use quieter gains in gameplay; the inspector auditions those event sequences.

Arc and Repulsor finish through their existing tails, with no extra expiry cue. Aegis has no sustained audio layer; its start and collapse leave room for propulsion.

All 23 WAVs in `assets/audio/sfx/` are live: seven road-device cues, eight equipped-ability cues, one physical ship-impact cue, five lap/finish announcements, one championship reward and one shared fire/lava contact texture. The eight equipment cues are generated first passes awaiting listening approval; their configs and exact trim/gain/fade edits are retained in `assets/audio/sfx/equipment-provenance.json`. Arc hull impact and target contact share `arc-hit.wav`. Aegis and Repulsor use the approved softened recordings. The race bus shares each announcement recording with Sounds, including lap/final lap and all three finish variants. Countdown/GO retain their original synthesized tones, levels and timing. Lap/finish announcements stay clear of the underwater filter; physical collisions use `ship-impact.wav` through the world mix. A shielded collision uses the Aegis block alone.

Original synthesis remains live for Vector activation/rush, Ion activation/wake/impact/confirmation, the approved Arc shots, propulsion and countdown/GO. Sample-backed cues have one source only; missing files stay silent and dev previews report the error. Unavailable pickups and Vector cancellation are silent, retaining HUD feedback. Ion emission ends by fading its bed over a few hundred milliseconds; there is no separate expiry chime. Ordinary Vector exit ends its rush without a separate cue. Aegis expiry/cancellation retain their warp collapse.

The **World contact** group has one **Fire · bonfire crackle** audition shared by Helion and Cindercoil. Sounds plays three seconds of contact followed by its 180 ms release, using shared effects volume and Play/Stop handling in both the menu and docked Ship inspector. To see the effects too, choose **Fire · heat + recovery** in the inspector's combined picker and press **Trigger**: four seconds of contact play the same sound alongside the continuous slow lava-red hull pulse, smoke and flames, then sound and visuals fade together over five seconds of recovery. Thunder and shelf events retain their original synthesis and are not separate entries in this picker.
