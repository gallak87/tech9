# Player ship source assets

Kestrel, Shrike, Bastion and Needle retain their approved silhouettes, authored component geometry and named exhaust anchors. Runtime meshes are exported from editable Blender sources, with original procedural PBR surfaces. No downloaded model or texture assets are used; generated concept images remain references only. Per-ship provenance records retain the original concept and approval history.

Rebuild from `games/vector-nova`:

```sh
blender --background --threads 6 --python assets/ships/kestrel/build_kestrel.py -- --toktx /path/to/toktx
blender --background --threads 6 --python assets/ships/build_fleet.py -- --toktx /path/to/toktx
```

Use Khronos KTX-Software 4.4.2. The fleet generator accepts `--ship bastion` (or `shrike` / `needle`) after `--` for an individual ship. Add `--render` for Blender review stills in ignored `.experiments/`. Sources are saved before export consolidation, preserving components, modifiers and editable procedural materials. Omitting `--toktx` exports a lossless PNG GLB for authoring; production assets use mipmapped UASTC KTX2.

For a fast material/geometry check, use `-- --ship shrike --preview`. This skips baking and writes `.experiments/fleet-material-preview/shrike/shrike.glb` without replacing the production GLB or editable source. Visual acceptance uses the exported asset in Three.js.

`clean_finish.py` supplies the shared clean coated ceramic, resin composite and machined-alloy finish through `crafted_finish`. The fleet additions also retain their blue-grey service ceramic and individual livery colors. This pass prioritizes broad reflections and material separation without adding wear or dirt masks. `surface_finish.py` provides the common baking helpers: color, roughness, tangent normals and short-range cavity occlusion are baked at 2048 px for main pearl ceramic and 512 px for the secondary families. Kestrel retains sixteen source maps and twelve runtime images; each fleet addition retains twenty source maps and fifteen runtime images, including its service-ceramic family. The stock glTF exporter packs roughness/occlusion together, and clearcoat reuses the existing normal image. Broad lighting remains dynamic; setup and inspection share a procedural 256px PMREM studio while tracks retain their own lighting.

Ceramic uses softer bevels than machined retaining lips. Bastion has individual aft shoulder plates and recessed transverse cleats that reveal the existing graphite skeleton while retaining its slim catamaran envelope. The three fleet additions have formed canopy coamings and gaskets; Kestrel has a strengthened structural seat and gasket. All nozzles keep real recessed throats and their original propulsion origins. Cold mounting cuffs and aft petals form two axial layers around a recessed dark pressure sleeve; two broad spring keepers bridge the overlap, with a bright machined retaining lip. Satin blue-grey service ceramic distinguishes nacelle saddles and selected panel families from the main pearl shell. Colored livery is painted rather than metallic, and resin-coated graphite remains distinct from the metal fittings. `propulsion_chamber.py` adds a single recessed central emitter and three supporting baffles to each nozzle. The small active face shares the existing Power material, so throttle and boost control it together with the surrounding aperture. Short-range cavity occlusion and narrow service-cowling gaskets reinforce sheltered joints. The dark canopies use tinted low-metallicity dielectric surfaces with a clear coat; Kestrel’s intake uses folded blades and readable projected service joints. Approved silhouettes, livery, hull envelopes and exhaust anchors remain the contract; exact exported dimensions, triangle counts, material groups and anchors are recorded in each asset manifest.
